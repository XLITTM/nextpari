# NextPari canonical sports catalog

The server-side entry point is [`server/sports/catalog/index.ts`](../server/sports/catalog/index.ts).
It takes reviewed catalog data and provider observations, and returns resolved events,
bookmaker offers and explicit mapping issues. It makes no network or database calls.
Canonical IDs are for catalog consumers. Existing provider fixture, market and outcome
references in stored bets and in the sportsbook acceptance and settlement contracts
retain their existing meaning.

## Identity

| Record | Identity and scope |
| --- | --- |
| Sport and country | Stable catalog IDs. A country may represent a sporting territory. |
| Competition and season | A competition belongs to one sport and may have a country. A season belongs to exactly one competition. |
| Participant | A stable sport-scoped identity for a team, individual or other entrant. Multiple teams may have identical names. |
| Event | A stable catalog ID with its sport, competition, optional season and approved kickoff, and exact participant roles. |
| Period | A sport-scoped stable ID with documented rules, such as football regulation excluding extra time, first half, or hockey including overtime. |
| Market | Event, period, market definition, optional subject, and exact line. Market definitions identify the metric, rules and outcome shape. |
| Selection | Market and exact outcome target. Home and away carry the verified participant ID. Multi-entrant markets use canonical participant IDs. |
| Bookmaker offer and odds | A market offer adds the provider, provider ID namespace and external bookmaker ID. Its odds ID adds the canonical selection. Snapshots also carry the upstream event, market, period, line, selection and optional odds-row IDs. |

IDs generated by the normalizer are opaque serialized tuples. Keep them as strings
and keep the identity fields intact. Prices and timestamps can change without changing
an odds ID. A rule change that changes the meaning of a period, market or selection
requires a distinct identity. Use a separate market definition for different measures,
rules, payout structures or outcome sets even if a provider displays the same label.

## Reviewed mappings and corrections

`CatalogDefinition` supplies canonical entities and `VerifiedAlias` crosswalks keyed by
provider, ID namespace, entity kind and exact external ID. Store a reviewable evidence
reference with each alias. Examples include a shared fixture ID supplied by an organizer,
a verified provider cross-reference, or a reviewed official schedule crosswalk.
The service loading the catalog owns the trust and persistence of that evidence.

Two providers resolve to a single event when their independently verified aliases point
to that event and both observations agree with its scope and participant roles.
Names and times alone cannot establish a match. When the catalog supplies a scheduled
kickoff, the observed kickoff must agree exactly. A recycled ID, an unexpected home/away
swap, a wrong league, or a conflicting alias stays unresolved. Even a plausible match
remains unresolved when the alias has multiple canonical targets.

Review a postponed fixture against the evidence, then update its approved kickoff while
retaining its canonical event ID. Replacing an incorrect alias requires a new catalog
snapshot and rebuilding the affected observations from source. Publish the new engine
and its normalized view together so stale offers from the old mapping are withdrawn.
Keep historical bet references in their existing provider-native form.

## Odds and market rules

`ProviderMarketRule` explicitly binds one provider namespace, bookmaker, external
period and external market ID to a canonical period and market definition. Register
only documented combinations. For fixed outcomes, each provider code maps to one
canonical outcome, independently of the outcome order and its displayed name.
Supply the provider participant ID as corroboration when a fixed home/away row has it.
For multi-entrant markets, resolve every participant ID through the reviewed crosswalk.
Player props resolve their subject to a confirmed event participant or eligible subject.
The built-in definitions cover 1X2, two-way moneyline, Over/Under and Asian Handicap.
Additional definitions can describe other sports, periods, props and outcome sets.

Pass original line text. The engine removes equivalent decimal padding and normalizes
equal-stake split components without floating-point rounding. It preserves the split
structure and distinguishes lines such as `2.5` and `2.5001`. Handicap rules must state
the sign convention relative to their canonical anchor. A single quarter line and its
split representation retain separate IDs. Feed-specific equivalences require verified
semantics before any future crosswalk treats them as interchangeable.

Each market observation is a complete selection snapshot for one line. A missing,
unknown, repeated or invalid selection makes the offer incomplete and clears its prices.
Suspensions and explicit closes replace older offers. Duplicate revisions with the
same identity and timestamp collapse when their odds agree. Equal-time contradictions
clear the offer until a newer revision arrives. Provider event lifecycle observations
suppress obsolete phases, and closed, finished or unknown events expose no selectable
odds. Source and receipt times are in UTC epoch milliseconds.

Call `marketAvailability` or `selectableOdds` at read time with the configured live and
prematch freshness limits. Refetching an unchanged offer cannot extend its freshness.
Missing or invalid source timestamps leave the offer stale. Use `latestMarketSnapshot`
only for offers with the same ID from the same catalog revision. It compares source
timestamps, using receipt time for ordering when a source timestamp is unavailable.

## Cursor integration for BetsAPI

Handoff reviewed against Draft PR #92 at `d63de586f4a0d3a2cf9d88533b81e2f0e5ae0d2d`.

1. Assemble reviewed `CatalogDefinition` data and `ProviderMarketRule` rows at the
   server feed ingestion boundary. Instantiate `SportsNormalizationEngine` once for
   that catalog revision.
2. Decode the BetsAPI event fields into `ProviderEvent`. In the inspected PR #92
   shape, `eventId`, `sportId`, `league.id`, `home.id`, `away.id`, `kickoff` and `status`
   correspond to the external event, sport, competition, side IDs, kickoff and status.
   Use the real receipt time. Supply optional country or season IDs only when the feed
   attests to the competition country or season. A home team's country is insufficient.
3. Build `ProviderMarket` rows at the BetsAPI decode boundary where the upstream
   bookmaker, period, market code, original line and exact selection identifiers are
   still available. Preserve the source update time and suspend/close status. PR #92's
   preview `NormalizedMarket` already drops bookmaker and period provenance and rounds
   lines. In `server/betsapi/client.ts`, `BetsApiReadOnlyClient.loadEvent` has the
   raw odds payload immediately before calling `normalizeFootballDetails`. Use that
   boundary, and the equivalent point before `normalizeFootballList` for list data.
   Leave rows unresolved when source identifiers or line semantics cannot be verified.
4. Call `engine.normalizeBatch(observations)` with the current source-event set and any
   updates being reconciled within that read. Publish the result as the catalog view.
   Log or queue `unresolvedEvents` and `unresolvedMarkets` for crosswalk review. One
   contradictory observation blocks its external event ID within the batch while other
   verified providers can still contribute. When a source withdraws an offer, send an
   explicit close or replace that source's current snapshot so the old offer disappears.
   A catalog revision change or an unresolved current source event requires dropping
   its previous offers from any caller-owned cache.
5. Read eligible quotes through `selectableOdds`. Preserve the snapshot's provider
   references for any handoff to `server/sports/betsapiQuote.ts`. This library supplies
   catalog and odds identity only. It does not authorize or settle bets.

Live use requires approved fixture and participant crosswalks, verified BetsAPI market
rules and the caller's persistent catalog/ingestion wiring. No production aliases,
database migration, credentials or runtime configuration are required to run this
branch's tests.

## League and team logos

`SportsLogoCatalog` consumes by-ID lookup responses and matches the exact returned ID
and entity kind against a reviewed alias. TheSportsDB v1 is preferred. API-Sports
Football is an optional fallback using its own ID namespace, with a URL that must
match the exact entity ID and kind. Missing assets, conflicting matches and unrelated
URLs produce no asset. Display a neutral placeholder when `get` returns no asset.

`STARTER_LOGO_CATALOG` includes opt-in reviewed TheSportsDB links for the Premier
League (4328) and Arsenal (133604). Merge the reviewed rows into the catalog by stable
ID. Other competitions and teams need reviewed crosswalks and by-ID lookup responses.
The engine does not send external requests or change the existing UI logo component.

Provider references

- [TheSportsDB v1 by-ID lookups](https://www.thesportsdb.com/docs/api)
- [API-Sports Football team and league lookups](https://www.api-football.com/documentation-v3)
